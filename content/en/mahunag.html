Why people come here

Of all the devtas of Karsog, none draws people like Mahunag. Families come from across Mandi district to pray, to make a vow, or to give thanks when a vow comes true. The main temple, Shri Mool Mahunag (“the original Mahunag”), stands at Bakhari Kothi among deodar forest and terraced fields, and it is the chief seat among the Mahunag shrines of the area.

Mahunag is also known as a devta of justice. Many people bring him their disputes, land quarrels and court cases, and ask for a fair outcome.

The legend of Karna

Local people believe that Mahunag is Karna, the warrior of the Mahabharata who never refused anyone who asked him for help. The story goes that after the war, Karna’s last rites were performed on the banks of the Sutlej near Tattapani, and that he later appeared in this valley as Mahunag, in the form of a nag (serpent) devta.

Karna gave away even his own armour. Here, people say, he still gives: fair judgement to anyone who asks.

The Mahunag Mela

The Shri Mool Mahunag Mela is the biggest fair of the Karsog valley. It is held in mid-May, usually about 14–19 May (15–19 May in 2026), starting around Jyeshtha Sankranti. Over five days the devta’s procession comes out with drums and horns, and there are folk music and nati dance nights, stalls and a community meal. If you can plan your trip around it, this is the valley at its most alive.

Dates follow the Hindu calendar and can move by a day or two, so check locally before you travel. See all the valley’s fairs on the melas calendar.

How to reach

  • By bus: HRTC buses leave Karsog bus stand for Shri Mool Mahunag at 11:00 AM and 2:10 PM. See Karsog to Mahunag bus timings.
  • By taxi: taxis wait at the Karsog taxi stand in the main market. For short trips they charge about ₹250 an hour including the first 10 km; agree the fare before you start.
  • By road: Mahunag is about 26 km from Karsog, about 1¼ hours by car. The road climbs through Churag; a rougher road comes back down via Chaira. Watch both routes in the videos below.
  • Temple day: many visitors see Mamleshwar Mahadev in the morning, then Kamaksha Devi at Kao, then Mahunag, and are back in Karsog by evening. A full day in a Dzire costs about ₹1,500–2,500.

Good to know

  • This is a working temple. Dress modestly, take off your shoes before the temple area, and ask before photographing any worship.
  • Carry some cash for offerings and snacks; there is no ATM at the temple.
  • Spring to autumn is the easiest time to visit. During the mela the roads and the temple are very crowded, so start early.
