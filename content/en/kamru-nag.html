The rain god of Mandi

Kamru Nag is worshipped across Mandi district as the god of rain, and people come here to pray for good rains and good harvests. Local people identify him with Barbarik, a warrior of the Mahabharata who, the story goes, watched the great war from a mountain top.

The small wooden temple stands beside the lake in a forest clearing. The water glints with coins and ornaments offered over centuries. These offerings belong to the devta: nothing is ever taken out of the lake.

The Kamru Nag Mela

The biggest day of the year is the Kamru Nag Mela in mid-June, when thousands of pilgrims walk up to the lake and tea stalls line the trail. It is the most atmospheric time to go, but expect crowds. The rest of the year the trail is quiet. Dates follow the local calendar, so check before you plan; see the melas calendar.

How to reach

  • To Rohanda: the trail starts at Rohanda, on the Karsog–Sundernagar road, about 52 km from Karsog (about 2¼ hours by car). Rohanda is also about 35 km from Sundernagar and 47 km from Mandi. Buses on the Karsog–Sundernagar road pass through Rohanda; ask the conductor.
  • The trek: about 6 km on a steep, well-used forest path, 3 to 4 hours up and about 2 hours down.
  • From Shikari Devi: experienced walkers can trek between Shikari Devi and Kamru Nag, a long day of about 16 km. Go with someone who knows the way.

Good to know

  • Respect the lake. Offerings go in; nothing comes out. Don’t wade in or touch the offerings.
  • Start early so you are back at Rohanda before dark, and carry water and food outside the mela season.
  • Wear good walking shoes and carry a warm layer and rain protection; weather changes fast at this height.
  • Mobile signal is unreliable on the trail. Download offline maps before you start.
