CAG audit · new07.10.2026

Jal Shakti Vibhag (IPH) Karsog Division — CAG inspection report 2022-24

Principal Accountant General (Audit), Himachal Pradesh · Period: August 2022 – December 2024 · Audit done 29.01.2025 – 04.02.2025 · Supplied under RTI AGHPS/R/E/26/00057, reply dated 07.10.2026

What the document says

  • ₹27.00 lakh paid on eight contractor bills (March and July 2024) whose mineral transit passes were “not found” on the Geology Department website or were in other people’s names. Audit says this indicates the passes were forged, and that the bills were passed against a High Court order (CWP 2395). The division replied that the bills were passed “unknowingly” (Para 1).
  • Water supply scheme for Karsog town to urban standard, ₹8.25 crore spent by December 2024: split into 10 contracts worth ₹7.15 crore without the competent authority’s permission; ₹24.00 lakh of work beyond the approved quantities without approval; ₹19.54 lakh of GI pipe charged to it but issued to other works; ₹1.00 crore of Jal Jeevan Mission material charged to it and later taken back (Para 11).
  • Lift irrigation scheme Chakhan–Tumman: ₹1.19 crore spent and not commissioned as of December 2024. Damaged in the July 2023 floods, with no restoration done. The work was split into 25 jobs (₹92.77 lakh) and final bills of ₹70.01 lakh were pending (Para 2).
  • ₹1.33 crore of works given to D-class contractors registered outside Mandi zone, against the HP IPH Enlistment Rules, 2013 (Para 3).
  • ₹40.00 lakh released for Karsog sewerage in September 2019 still lying unused. Audit notes that Karsog has no sewerage system and that effluents end up in the river (Para 4).
  • ₹15.61 lakh of SDRF/NDRF relief money spent on paving the Ghangoti–Dabru Dawar link road — work not covered by SDRF norms (Para 6).
More findings in this report (6)
  • Tender sale and tender opening registers without contractors’ signatures, the L1 bidder or earnest money details; no agreement signed by the Executive Engineer in the agreement register (Para 10).
  • 22,279 water samples taken (April 2022 – December 2024) with no register of signatures to verify where they came from; no microbiologist in either sub-divisional lab (Para 7).
  • ₹32.45 lakh outstanding under Miscellaneous Works Advances, including “wrong payments” against three Assistant Engineers (Para 8).
  • ₹12.92 lakh of water charges and other receipts deposited in the treasury 7 to 162 days late (Para 5).
  • 6 of 10 Junior Engineer posts and the whole drawing branch vacant (Para 9).
  • Paras of earlier reports since 2010-11 still marked “compliance awaited” — for example ₹2,240.56 lakh of unfruitful expenditure on LWSS Sarror Khad to Churag & Tattapani (2022-23, Para 1).

Keep in mind: Inspection report paras are audit observations. The division confirmed the facts and figures in most paras; they stay open until audit accepts its reply. The annexure listing the 25 split jobs was not supplied. The covering letter says the report has not been published under Section 4.

HPSEBL Electrical Division Karsog — CAG inspection report 2010-22

Principal Accountant General (Audit), Himachal Pradesh · Period: April 2010 – March 2022 · Audit done May – June 2022 · Supplied under RTI AGHPS/R/E/26/00058, reply dated 08.10.2026 · The copy supplied is headed “Draft inspection report”

What the document says

  • 44 works worth ₹4.20 crore executed by consumers themselves (2017-22) never taken over as HPSEBL assets, so they are not in the asset list, not depreciated and not claimed in tariff (Para 1).
  • ₹2.67 crore of electricity bills outstanding at the end of March 2022 — almost double the ₹1.37 crore of March 2019. ₹1.72 crore of it was owed by the IPH department (Para 2).
  • Low-voltage scheme: ₹2.40 crore spent on LT lines, but the work could not be completed because HPSEBL did not supply the 63 kVA transformers and fittings (Para 3).
  • Chhatri 33/22 kV sub-station: ₹7.18 crore spent against ₹6.88 crore approved. Due by 31 July 2021, still not complete in May 2022 (Para 10).
  • Power connection for the drinking-water scheme LWSS Kashapadi Jujer Khad not given more than 3 years after the IPH department deposited ₹22.66 lakh (Para 4).
  • 46–57% of distribution-transformer meters defective (2020-22). Losses (AT&C) of 24–31% against a 10% target (Paras 12, 13).
More findings in this report (6)
  • Security deposits of 20 consumers short by ₹13.69 lakh (Para 5).
  • ₹6.84 lakh spent beyond the deposit on the power connection for LWSS Chaira Khud to Dhamoon, not recovered (Para 6).
  • ₹5.12 lakh of material lying unused in store for one to five years, including a repaired 250 kVA transformer (Para 7).
  • A Tata 407 pickup bought in October 1996 not condemned (Para 8).
  • HT/LT line ratio 1:2.7 against the 1:1 norm (Para 11).
  • Staff: 126 of 278 posts vacant (31 March 2021).

Keep in mind: The previous audit of this division covered 2009-10, so this report spans 12 years. The annexure listing the 44 works was not supplied. We have blacked out electricity consumer account numbers. The covering letter says the report has not been published under Section 4.

HRTC Regional Office Karsog — CAG inspection report 2019-23

Principal Accountant General (Audit), Himachal Pradesh · Period: April 2019 – March 2023 · Audit done 04.03.2024 – 11.03.2024 · Supplied under RTI AGHPS/R/E/26/00059, reply dated 07.10.2026

What the document says

  • ₹62.58 lakh of HP Special Road Tax, already collected from passengers along with the fare, not paid to the government since February 2017. The 2017-19 audit had found ₹27.38 lakh unpaid (OBS-1253354).
  • The Karsog workshop has no effluent treatment plant and no consent to operate from the HP Pollution Control Board, with a khad flowing next to it (OBS-1255708).
  • The new Karsog bus stand (₹1.99 crore allocated, opened 18.09.2022) was designed without a septic tank. HRTC now uses the Nagar Panchayat’s tank, built two toilets for the Nagar Panchayat in return, and paid ₹1.56 lakh for emptying the tank (OBS-1256065).
  • ₹25.66 lakh of interest paid because leave encashment and gratuity were paid late (OBS-1254694).
  • No internal audit after 31.12.2019, and no physical verification of assets after 12.11.2021 (OBS-1255707).
  • Earnings per kilometre below target: a ₹38.27 crore shortfall for 2019-23. ₹1.14 crore spent on extra diesel because mileage targets were missed (OBS-1254697, OBS-1254698).
More findings in this report (4)

Why this page exists: Section 4 of the RTI Act

Most of this information should already be on government websites. It usually isn’t.

Section 4(1)(b) of the RTI Act, 2005 requires every public authority to publish, on its own and without anyone asking, 17 kinds of information and to update it every year. These include its budget and how the money was spent, its schemes and who benefited, the permits and concessions it gave, and the details of its officers. Section 4(2) says authorities should keep publishing as much as they can, so that people need to file RTIs as little as possible. Section 4(4) says it must be easy to get, in the local language.

In practice, Karsog offices rarely do this. In the three audit replies of October 2026, the audit office itself wrote that the inspection reports have not been published under Section 4. Nagar Panchayat Karsog wrote the same about the Ward 3 park records. That is why people have to file RTIs one by one, and why we publish the replies here.

Ask for the Section 4 link in every RTI

Add a line such as: “Web link where this information is disclosed under Section 4 of the RTI Act, or state that it is not.” The copy-ready texts below already include it. A written “not published” is useful in an appeal or complaint.

  1. Pick the right portal above and register with your email and mobile number.
  2. Choose the department and office. Write what you want clearly, point by point. Ask for PDF by email — Section 7(9) says information should be given in the form asked for.
  3. Pay the ₹10 fee online. BPL card holders pay no fee (Section 7(5)); upload the BPL certificate.

Frequently asked

Is it legal to publish these documents?

Yes. They were supplied by public offices under the RTI Act, 2005, or published by the audit department. The Act puts no restriction on how a citizen uses information once it has been supplied. We have removed the applicant’s phone number, email and address.

Does an audit objection mean money was stolen?

No. An audit para records what auditors found in the files — missing records, rules not followed, money lying unused. The office gets to explain, and paras are dropped once audit accepts the reply. Some paras point to serious problems; others are settled with paperwork. Read them as questions the office has to answer.

How do I get the latest audit report of my panchayat or office?

Use one of the copy-ready texts above. Gram Panchayats, Nagar Panchayats and college funds are audited by the HP State Audit Department. Most other government offices are audited by the AG (Audit) Himachal Pradesh, which is part of the CAG.

Can I use these documents in my own RTI, complaint or appeal?

Yes. Quote the letter number and date printed on the document. It also shows the office that the same kind of information has already been released.

Why are some parts blacked out?

Only personal details: the applicant’s phone number, email and home address, and bank account or electricity consumer numbers printed in a few reports. Nothing else in the documents has been changed.
