Temple guide

Kamaksha Devi, Kao

One of the main goddess temples of the Karsog valley, in the village of Kao. The carved temple and its village make a lovely short trip from town, and at Navratri it is the busiest place in the valley.

Kao, 7 km from KarsogBusiest at NavratriOpen to all, no entry fee
Directions

The goddess of Kao

Kao’s temple is dedicated to Durga in her form as Maa Kamaksha. It is one of the most important goddess temples of the valley, and its carved wooden architecture and village setting make it one of Karsog’s most photographed places.

Local people believe the sage Parashurama installed the goddess here, and that she was once the family goddess of the old Suket kingdom, whose kings ruled this area.

Navratri at Kao

The temple is at its most festive during the two Navratris: Chaitra Navratri (March–April) and Sharadiya Navratri (September–October). Ashtami night is especially busy, with special worship, music and crowds from across the valley. See the melas calendar for dates.

How to reach

  • By road: Kao is about 7 km from Karsog bus stand along the Karsog–Mamel–Kao road, about 20 minutes by car. You pass Mamleshwar Mahadev at Mamel on the way.
  • By taxi: taxis wait at the Karsog taxi stand in the main market. Short local trips cost about ₹250 an hour including the first 10 km.
  • Temple day: Mamleshwar, Kao and Mahunag make a good one-day temple circuit from Karsog.

Good to know

  • Dress modestly and take off your shoes before entering the temple area.
  • Carry some cash for offerings; there is no ATM in the village.
  • For a quiet visit, avoid Navratri; for the festive atmosphere, go on Ashtami.

FAQ

Common questions

How far is Kamaksha Devi temple from Karsog?
About 7 km from Karsog bus stand by the Karsog–Mamel–Kao road, about 20 minutes by car.
Which goddess is worshipped at Kao?
Durga, as Maa Kamaksha. Local tradition says she was installed by the sage Parashurama and was the family goddess of the old Suket kingdom.
When is the best time to visit Kamaksha Devi temple?
Navratri (March–April and September–October) is the most festive time, especially the night of Ashtami. For a quiet visit, any clear day is good.
Is there an entry fee?
No. The temple is open to all and offerings are voluntary.
