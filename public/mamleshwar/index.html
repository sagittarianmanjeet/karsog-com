Mamleshwar Mahadev —
the fire that never went out

Two kilometres from Karsog bazaar stands a Shiva temple that has watched over the valley since roughly the 7th century — keeping inside it a fire said never to have been extinguished, a drum built for a Pandava, and a single impossible grain of wheat.

Three wonders

Mamleshwar Mahadev is the spiritual anchor of Karsog Valley, and its fame rests on three artefacts kept within the temple:

  • The Akhand Dhuna — a sacred fire that, by tradition, has burned without interruption since the Pandavas lit it during their exile. Priests tend it to this day.
  • Bhima's drum — a six-foot drum (Bhekhli) of rhinoceros hide, said to have been played by Bhima, the strongest of the five brothers.
  • The grain of wheat — a single preserved grain of extraordinary size, around 200 g, held by tradition to be 5,000 years old and from the Mahabharata era.

Whatever one makes of the legends, the temple itself is a fine example of hill architecture, and its priests are generous with the stories if you visit at a quiet hour.

The Pandava connection

Karsog's name and half its shrines trace back to the Mahabharata: the valley is held to be one of the places the Pandavas passed their years of exile. Mamleshwar is the centre of that tradition — the Dhuna they lit, the drum Bhima beat, and the wheat from their fields all anchor the story physically in one courtyard.

The temple's biggest day is Maha Shivratri (February–March), when special worship draws crowds from across the valley — see the festival calendar.

How to reach

The temple is barely 2 km from Karsog main bazaar — an easy walk, or a few minutes by taxi (local rate ~₹250/hour including first 10 km, rate card). Reaching Karsog itself: HRTC buses from Mandi and Shimla — see Mandi bus timings and Shimla bus timings.

Temple circuit: most visitors do Mamleshwar, Kamaksha Devi (Kao) and Mahunag in one day — full-day Dzire ~₹1,500–2,500. Morning first: light is best and the courtyard is quietest.

Frequently asked

Is there an entry fee?

No. The temple is an active shrine, open to all. Modest dress; shoes off before entering.

Can I photograph the artefacts?

Ask the priests first — photography of the outer temple is generally fine, but the sanctum and artefacts may be restricted.

How long does a visit take?

30–60 minutes for the temple itself; half a day if you combine it with Kamaksha Devi and Mahunag on the temple circuit.

Where can I stay?

Karsog bazaar hotels, the HPTDC Mamleshwar Resort at Chindi (named after this temple), or homestays — see where to stay.
