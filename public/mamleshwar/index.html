Mamleshwar Mahadev

An ancient Shiva temple at Mamel, a short walk from Karsog town. Tradition links it to the Pandavas, and inside you can see a sacred fire, a huge old drum and a grain of wheat that people believe date from their time.

Mamel, 2 km from Karsog bus standMahashivratri & Nalwar MelaOpen to all, no entry fee
Directions

The temple

Mamleshwar Mahadev is Karsog’s own Shiva temple, in Mamel village at the edge of town. It is built in the old hill style of wood and stone, and it is the spiritual centre of many of the valley’s festivals: the spring Nalwar Mela opens with Mamleshwar’s procession.

No one knows exactly how old the temple is. Local tradition says it goes back to the time of the Mahabharata.

What to see inside

Mamleshwar is famous for three things kept in the temple. Each comes with a story that people here hold dear:

  • The sacred fire (dhuna): a fire that, by tradition, has been kept burning since the Pandavas lit it during their exile. Priests tend it every day.
  • Bhima’s drum: a very large old drum that people say was played by Bhima, the strongest of the five Pandava brothers.
  • The grain of wheat: an unusually large grain of wheat that people believe comes from the Pandavas’ time.

Whatever you make of the stories, the priests are happy to tell them if you visit at a quiet hour.

Festivals

  • Mahashivratri (February or March): the biggest night of the year, with worship through the night and a community meal.
  • Shravan Mondays (July–August): special worship every Monday of the holy month.
  • Nalwar Mela (usually 5–11 April, not held every year): Karsog’s spring fair begins with Mamleshwar’s procession.
  • Budhi Diwali (about a month after Diwali): the hill Diwali, celebrated here with night-long festivities.

How to reach

  • On foot: Mamel is about 2 km from Karsog bus stand along the Karsog–Mamel–Kao road, an easy half-hour walk.
  • By taxi: about 10 minutes from the Karsog taxi stand. Short local trips cost about ₹250 an hour including the first 10 km.
  • Temple day: Mamleshwar in the morning, then Kamaksha Devi at Kao (a few kilometres up the same road) and Mahunag, back in Karsog by evening.

Good to know

  • Dress modestly and take off your shoes before entering.
  • Ask the priests before taking photos inside; rules for the sanctum and the old objects can vary.
  • Early morning is the quietest and the light is best.

FAQ

Common questions

How far is Mamleshwar Mahadev from Karsog?
About 2 km from Karsog bus stand, at Mamel village: a half-hour walk or about 10 minutes by taxi.
What is special about Mamleshwar Mahadev temple?
Tradition links the temple to the Pandavas. Inside are a sacred fire that people say has burned since their time, a large drum said to be Bhima’s, and a big old grain of wheat believed to date from the Mahabharata age.
How old is Mamleshwar Mahadev temple?
Its exact age is not known. Local tradition places it in the time of the Mahabharata.
Is there an entry fee?
No. The temple is open to everyone; offerings are voluntary.
When is the best time to visit?
Any time of year. Mahashivratri (February–March), Shravan Mondays (July–August) and the Nalwar Mela in April are the most festive times.
