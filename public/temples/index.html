Valley of temples

Temples of Karsog

The temples most worth visiting in the valley: the story behind each, when to go and tips for your visit.

10 templesFair dates on each

Karsog is known as a valley of temples. Most are built in the old Himachali style of carved wood and stone, and each village has its own devta.

These are the temples most worth visiting, with the story behind each, the best time to go and a few tips. Stories and legends are told here as local people tell them.

Temple by temple

10 temples to visit

Shri Mool Mahunag Templeश्री मूल माहूँनाग मंदिर

Bakhari Kothi (Mahunag), about 26 km from Karsog

The best-known temple of the Karsog valley, dedicated to Mahunag, who is worshipped here as Karna, the generous warrior of the Mahabharata. People from all over Mandi district come here to pray, and the temple hosts the valley’s biggest fair every May.

Locals believe that after the Mahabharata war, Karna’s last rites were performed near Tattapani on the Sutlej, and that the devta later appeared here as Mahunag.

When to go

  • Mid-May, during the Mahunag Mela, for the fair, music and crowds
  • Any time from spring to autumn for a quieter visit

Fairs

  • Shri Mool Mahunag Mela: mid-May (about 14–19 May), a five-day fair with folk music and dance

Tips

  • Dress modestly and take off your shoes before entering the temple area
  • Combine it with Mamleshwar Mahadev and Kamaksha Devi for a one-day temple circuit

Mamleshwar Mahadev Templeममलेश्वर महादेव मंदिर

Mamel village, about 2 km from Karsog bus stand

An ancient Shiva temple built in the traditional wood-and-stone style, right next to Karsog town. It is known for the unusual old objects kept inside, and it leads Karsog’s main spring fair.

Tradition says the Pandavas stayed here during their exile. Inside you can see a large drum said to be Bhima’s, a sacred fire that locals say has been burning since that time, and a huge old grain of wheat believed to date from the Pandavas.

When to go

  • Mahashivratri (February–March) and Shravan Mondays (July–August) for special worship
  • April, when the Nalwar Mela opens with the temple’s procession

Fairs

  • Nalwar Mela: 5–11 April in most years (not held every year)
  • Mahashivratri: February or March
  • Budhi Diwali: about a month after Diwali

Tips

  • Easy to walk to from Karsog town
  • Photography rules inside the temple can vary; ask before taking pictures

Kamaksha Devi Temple, Kaoकामाक्षा देवी मंदिर, काओ

Kao village, about 7 km from Karsog

One of the main goddess temples of the valley, dedicated to Durga as Maa Kamaksha. The carved wooden temple in a quiet village setting is a lovely short trip from Karsog town.

Locals believe the sage Parashurama installed the goddess here, and that she was once the family goddess of the old Suket kingdom.

When to go

  • Navratri (March–April and September–October), the busiest and most festive time
  • Any clear day for a quieter visit

Fairs

  • Navratri celebrations: twice a year, March–April and September–October

Tips

  • Pair it with Mamleshwar Mahadev and Mahunag for a temple day
  • Carry cash for small offerings; there are no ATMs in the village

Shikari Devi Templeशिकारी देवी मंदिर

Shikari peak, about 3,359 m, above Janjehli

A goddess temple on the highest peak of Mandi district, with sweeping Himalayan views and forest all around. It is famous for having no roof.

Locals believe the Pandavas worshipped the goddess here during their exile, and that every attempt to build a roof over the shrine has failed. Many also say snow never settles on the idol.

When to go

  • April to June and September to November
  • The road usually closes in winter because of snow

Fairs

  • Navratri gatherings: March–April and September–October, when the road is open

Tips

  • It is cold at the top even in summer; carry a warm layer and rain protection
  • Check the weather and road before you go

Kamru Nag Temple & Lakeकमरूनाग मंदिर व झील

High in the forest above Rohanda, about 3,334 m

A temple beside a small sacred lake deep in deodar forest, reached on foot. Kamru Nag is worshipped across Mandi district as the god of rain.

Locals identify Kamru Nag with Barbarik, a warrior of the Mahabharata. Devotees offer coins and jewellery into the lake as a sign of faith.

When to go

  • Late spring to autumn, when the trail is open
  • Mid-June for the big annual fair at the lake

Fairs

  • Kamru Nag Mela: mid-June, at the lake

Tips

  • Wear good walking shoes; the last part is a forest trek
  • Offerings in the lake are sacred; don’t touch or remove anything

Chindi Mata Templeचिंडी माता मंदिर

Chindi, on the Karsog–Shimla road: about 7 km from Karsog by the short road, 15 km by the main road

A beautifully carved goddess temple among apple orchards and deodar trees at Chindi, a popular stop on the Karsog–Shimla road with an HPTDC hotel nearby.

Locals say the goddess showed a priest the plan of the temple through a line of ants, and that the name Chindi comes from “chinti”, the word for ant.

When to go

  • 2–4 August for the Chindi Mata Mela
  • Spring and autumn for orchard views and clear skies

Fairs

  • Chindi Mata Mela: 2–4 August every year

Tips

  • Good sunset views nearby
  • Easy to combine with a drive to Tattapani or Shimla

Mahamaya Temple, Pangnaमहामाया मंदिर, पांगणा

Pangna, about 19 km from Karsog

An old goddess temple on the top floor of the historic Pangna Fort tower, still in daily worship. The fort is one of the oldest buildings of the area, built in the traditional Kath-Kuni style of timber and stone that stands up well to earthquakes.

Pangna was an early seat of the Suket rulers, and the temple and fort tower are among the area’s most important heritage sites.

When to go

  • Any time of year; early June for the local Katal Mela

Fairs

  • Katal Mela: early June (about 8–11 June)

Tips

  • See the fort tower and old houses of Pangna village
  • Combine with a drive via Churag

Laxmi Narayan Temple, Karsogलक्ष्मी नारायण मंदिर, करसोग

Purana Bazaar (old market), Karsog town

A temple in the heart of Karsog’s old bazaar, with an evening aarti every day. It is easy to visit while walking around town.

When to go

  • Janmashtami (August–September), when the old bazaar celebrates

Fairs

  • Janmashtami: August or September

Tips

  • Visit at aarti time in the evening

Nag Chawasi Siddh Temple, Mahogनाग च्वासी सिद्ध मंदिर, महोग

Mahog, in the Chawasi area of Karsog

The temple of Nag Chawasi Siddh, the main devta of the Chawasi area, in a village setting in the hills of Karsog.

Locals believe the old Suket kings honoured the devta with gifts that are still kept at the temple.

When to go

  • 12–15 May for the Chawasi Utsav at Nahvindhar
  • About a month after Diwali for Budhi Diwali at Mahog

Fairs

  • Chawasi Utsav (Nahvindhar Thirshu Mela): 12–15 May
  • Budhi Diwali: about a month after Diwali

Tips

  • Ask locally about road conditions before driving up

Nag Dhamuni Temple, Seri Bungalowनाग धमूनी मंदिर, सेरी बंगलो

Seri Bungalow, near Karsog

The temple of Nag Dhamuni, one of the most important devtas of the Karsog valley, at Seri Bungalow.

Locals believe Nag Dhamuni came to the Karsog side from the Kullu hills and made this area his home.

When to go

  • The Seri Bhanju Mela in late August or September

Fairs

  • Seri Bhanju Mela: starts on Rishi Panchami (late August or September), three days

Tips

  • See the drone photos of Seri Bungalow on this site before you go

FAQ

Common questions

Which is the most famous temple in Karsog?
The Shri Mool Mahunag temple at Bakhari Kothi, about 26 km from Karsog, where Mahunag is worshipped as Karna. It hosts the valley’s biggest fair in mid-May.
Which temples can I see in one day from Karsog?
Mamleshwar Mahadev at Mamel, Kamaksha Devi at Kao and Mahunag make a good one-day temple circuit, back in Karsog by evening.
Why does the Shikari Devi temple have no roof?
Local people believe the goddess does not want a roof and that every attempt to build one has failed.
