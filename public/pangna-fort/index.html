Pangna Fort —
seven storeys of wood and stone

Before Suket's kings ruled from Sundernagar, they ruled from here. Built around 1211 AD, the tower fort of Pangna rises 60 feet in alternating layers of deodar and stone — the Kath-Kuni technique that has shrugged off eight centuries of Himalayan earthquakes.

The first capital of Suket

The Suket dynasty — one of the oldest princely lines of the western Himalaya — made Pangna its seat when the state was founded, and the tower fort was both palace and refuge. As the capital later moved, Pangna kept its tower, its temple and its role as the area's cultural heart.

The fort's top floor houses the Mahamaya Devi temple, still in active worship — which is why the tower has never been abandoned. The goddess, in a real sense, kept the fort alive.

Why Kath-Kuni matters

Kath-Kuni construction alternates courses of stone with beams of deodar, without mortar. The joints flex instead of cracking, which is why the tower has outlasted earthquakes that brought down newer masonry buildings. Pangna Fort is one of the finest surviving examples of the technique — worth the trip for anyone interested in Himalayan vernacular architecture.

Visiting the tower: the temple floor is an active shrine and access depends on the priests and custodians. Ask politely in the village; modest dress, shoes off at the temple level.

How to reach & where to stay

Pangna makes a comfortable day trip from Karsog — a full-day taxi (Dzire) runs roughly ₹1,800–3,000; see the rate card. Reaching Karsog: HRTC buses from Mandi and from Shimla.

  • PWD Rest House, Pangna — clean, basic government accommodation with historic charm; book via the SDM office, subject to availability.
  • Sarvpoornam Villa — a homestay at Pangna, good for groups, with home-cooked Himachali food.
  • Combine with the temple circuit or Chindi for a fuller day — see itineraries.

Frequently asked

Is there an entry fee?

No. The fort and temple are free to visit; offerings at the temple are voluntary.

How much time does it take?

An hour or two at the fort and village; half a day including the drive from Karsog either way.

Is photography allowed?

Of the exterior, yes — it's the valley's most photogenic structure. Inside the temple, ask first.

Is the road to Pangna good?

A normal hill road, fine for sedans in dry weather. In the monsoon check conditions locally and see the road-status banner on the homepage.
