Tattapani —
hot water on a cold river

"Tatta pani" means exactly what it promises: hot water. Natural sulphur springs emerge beside the Sutlej river roughly halfway between Karsog and Shimla — a winter bathing tradition centuries old, with a famous Makar Sankranti fair and a rafting stretch in season.

The springs

Sulphur-rich hot water surfaces along the Sutlej's banks at Tattapani, and bathing here is an old tradition — the water is believed locally to ease joint pain and skin ailments. The springs are at their most inviting from October to March, when the air is cold and the river valley fills with winter sun.

One honest note: after the Kol Dam reservoir raised the river level, most of the original open ghat springs went under water. Today bathing is mostly in tanks and pools maintained by guesthouses and resorts, usually for a fee. The experience is still worth the drive — just don't expect the old open riverbank ghats, and ask locally about current arrangements.

Tattapani also connects to Karsog's own mythology: it is said to be where Karna's last rites were performed — the same story that ends with his manifestation at Mahunag Temple in the valley above.

Makar Sankranti & rafting

The biggest day of Tattapani's year is Makar Sankranti in mid-January, when thousands come for a holy dip and the riverside fair. A ritual khichdi is cooked in enormous quantities — including a record-setting one in 2020. If you visit during the mela, arrive early; the road fills quickly.

In season, a rafting stretch operates on the Sutlej here — a gentler run suited to first-timers. Availability depends on water levels and operators, so confirm locally before promising the kids.

How to reach

  • From Karsog: ~50 km on the Shimla road, 1.5–2 hours. Day-trip taxi ~₹1,200–1,800 (Dzire). Shimla-bound HRTC buses pass through — see bus timings.
  • From Shimla: ~50 km via Naldehra, about 2 hours — one of the most popular short drives out of Shimla.
  • Combine with Chindi: the HPTDC café and sunset point at Chindi sits on the Karsog–Tattapani road; see the 1-day itinerary.
Monsoon note: the Shimla–Tattapani–Karsog road can develop slow patches in heavy rain. Check the road status on the homepage banner before travelling in July–September.

Frequently asked

Are the springs open all year?

Yes, but the hot water is most enjoyable October–March. Summer visits are pleasant for the river; the monsoon is best avoided for road reasons.

Is bathing free?

Mostly no — since the Kol Dam reservoir submerged the open ghats, bathing is largely in tanks at guesthouses and resorts, which charge. Ask locally about any free public access.

Can I stay overnight?

Tattapani has riverside guesthouses and resorts across budgets. Many Karsog visitors do it as a day trip instead — see where to stay in Karsog.
