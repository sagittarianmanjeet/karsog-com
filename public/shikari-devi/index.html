Shikari Devi Trek —
the roofless summit

At 3,359 m, Shikari Devi is the highest peak of Mandi district — crowned by an open-sky temple where, legend says, snow never settles inside the sanctum. The trail from Janjehli is one of Himachal's most rewarding short treks.

The temple and its legend

Shikari Devi — "goddess of hunters" — has been worshipped on this summit since long before roads reached the valley. Hunters once prayed here for success before entering the forests below; today's pilgrims climb for the goddess and for the 360° Himalayan panorama on a clear day.

The shrine is famously roofless. Local tradition holds that every attempt to build a roof has failed, and that snow — which lies deep across the summit in winter — never settles inside the sanctum itself. The temple is linked in local lore to the Pandavas, who are said to have worshipped the goddess during their exile.

How to reach

From Karsog: drive to Janjehli, roughly 40 km away (1.5–2 hours on a hill road via Chhatri). A full-day taxi from the Karsog stand costs about ₹2,000–2,500 for an Ertiga or Innova. Local buses also run between Karsog and Janjehli — verify times at the bus stand the evening before.

The trek: from Janjehli, the main trail climbs 5–6 km through dense deodar and spruce forest — allow 2.5–4 hours up and around 2 hours down. In dry months a rough vehicle track gets close to the top, so ask locally whether jeeps are currently running if trekking isn't for you.

  • Start early. Morning views are clearest; afternoon cloud often swallows the summit.
  • Carry water and snacks. There are no reliable shops on the trail; a dhaba may operate near the top in season.
  • No fuel, no ATM beyond Janjehli — fill up and withdraw cash in Karsog.
  • Mobile signal is weak or absent on the trail. Download offline maps before leaving Karsog.
Weather warning: conditions change fast above 3,000 m. December–March brings deep snow; July–August monsoon makes the trail slippery with near-zero visibility. Check locally in Janjehli before climbing, and keep the emergency numbers saved.

Frequently asked

How difficult is the trek?

Moderate — a steady 5–6 km forest climb with no technical sections. Reasonably fit beginners manage it; the altitude is felt near the top.

Can I drive to the temple?

In dry months a rough track approaches the summit and local jeeps sometimes run. It's weather-dependent — ask in Janjehli, and don't count on it after rain or snow.

Is there accommodation?

Janjehli has small hotels, a PWD rest house and homestays. Most trekkers stay there and do the summit as a half-day round trip. See where to stay.

Can I combine it with Kamru Nag?

Yes — a common 2-day plan does Shikari Devi from Janjehli on day one and the Kamru Nag lake trek on day two. See the ready-made itineraries.
