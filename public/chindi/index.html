Why stop at Chindi

Chindi sits at the point where the Karsog–Shimla road climbs out of the valley — which gives it its two assets: the long westward view that makes its sunset point famous, and a cooler, orchard-covered hillside that produces some of the valley's best apples.

In September–October the orchards are at their busiest and most beautiful; roadside stalls sell fruit straight from the trees. The HPTDC café makes the classic halt on the Tattapani day trip — hot springs first, Chindi sunset on the way home.

Where to stay

  • HPTDC Mamleshwar Resort — the reliable government-run property: valley views, comfortable rooms, in-house restaurant. Particularly good value off-season. Named for the great Mamleshwar Mahadev temple near Karsog.
  • PWD Rest House, Chindi — basic and clean at government rates; civilians book through the SDM office subject to availability.
Book ahead in apple season (September–October) — Chindi fills up faster than Karsog town. See all valley options on where to stay.

How to reach

Chindi is on the main Karsog–Shimla road: every Shimla–Karsog bus passes through it, and a taxi from Karsog takes about half an hour. The classic 1-day plan combines Tattapani's hot springs with the Chindi sunset — taxi ~₹1,200–1,800 (Dzire); see itineraries.

Frequently asked

Is Chindi worth an overnight stay?

Yes, especially if Karsog town accommodation is full or you want the quietest orchard setting in the valley. It's also a sensible last stop before the drive to Shimla.

When do the apples ripen?

The harvest peaks in September–October — the best time for both the orchards and the weather.

Does it snow in Chindi?

Occasionally in December–February. Roads normally stay open, but check the road-status banner on the homepage in winter.

Good to know

Locals say the goddess showed a priest the plan of the temple through a line of ants, and that the name Chindi comes from “chinti”, the word for ant.
