Kamru Nag —
the lake of offered gold

A sacred high-altitude lake at 3,334 m dedicated to Kamru Nag, the rain god of Mandi. For centuries devotees have dropped coins, ornaments and gold into its waters — a treasure that belongs to the deity and is never touched.

The rain god of Mandi

Kamru Nag is revered across Mandi district as the god of rain — villages send delegations here in dry years, and his blessing is sought before sowing seasons. Local tradition links the deity to the Mahabharata: he is identified with a great warrior who was persuaded to watch the war from this peak rather than fight in it.

The small wooden temple stands beside the lake in a forest clearing. What draws every visitor's eye is the water itself: glinting with coins and ornaments accumulated over centuries of offerings. Taking anything from the lake is considered a grave offence against the deity — and locals will tell you stories about what happened to those who tried.

How to reach

From Karsog: the trailhead is at Rohanda, on the Mandi–Karsog road roughly 30–35 km from Karsog (under an hour by road). A return taxi from the Karsog stand costs about ₹900–1,400 for a Dzire. Any Mandi-bound bus can also drop you at Rohanda — check times on the bus timings page.

The trek: from Rohanda a well-used forest trail climbs about 6 km to the lake. Allow 2.5–3.5 hours up through oak and deodar forest, and around 2 hours down. During the June mela the trail fills with pilgrims and temporary tea stalls appear; the rest of the year it is quiet.

  • Respect the lake. Offerings go in; nothing comes out. Photography is fine, wading is not.
  • Carry water and food outside mela season — there are no permanent shops on the trail.
  • Signal is unreliable beyond Rohanda. Download offline maps in Karsog.
  • Start by early morning to be back at Rohanda before the last buses pass.
Mela note: the Kamrunag Mela around 13–15 June draws thousands — the most atmospheric time to visit, but expect crowds, and book Karsog accommodation ahead. Dates shift with the local calendar; confirm locally.

Frequently asked

How hard is the trek?

Easy to moderate — about 6 km of steady forest climbing on a clear trail. Families with children do it during the mela.

Can the coins in the lake be collected?

No. The offerings are the deity's property by centuries-old tradition, and removing anything is strictly taboo — treated locally as theft from the god himself.

Is there anywhere to stay at the lake?

No permanent accommodation at the lake. Stay in Karsog or Janjehli and do it as a day trip. See where to stay.

Can I combine it with Shikari Devi?

Yes — the classic 2-day plan pairs the Shikari Devi trek with Kamru Nag. See the itineraries.
